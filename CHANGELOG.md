# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Scene viewer, laid out like REDkit's scene editor**: the screenplay, starting conditions, definitions and files above; the flow below as a graph (laid out as in REDkit for the game's own scenes when REDkit is installed) and the dialog sets with where each actor stands; and at the side the picked part's properties (from REDkit's copy), the scene's cameras, every animation its timelines play, and a section's language versions. Choices show each option numbered, with what it needs or does, and the options the game picks out are highlighted. Read through, playing lines, search and Back work as before. **Timeline** shows a section's lines, camera cuts, lights and fades, and each actor's body animations, faces and looks along it, as REDkit's timeline does, with an event's properties shown when it's clicked; **Play** runs the section and says each line when it comes (from part way through a line when it was paused there), and cutscene sections show their lines at the times the cutscene says them. **Preview** plays the section in 3D in time with the timeline: the actors put together from their entities and stood where the dialog set puts them, in the idles the game's tables give how they stand, with the section's body animations, changes of stance, additive gestures, walks, placements and visibility; their faces moved as the game moves them (expressions, blinks, nods and each line's lip sync from the speech files); and look-ats; seen through the section's cameras as they cut and blend (along each blend's curves, turning the short way round), with subtitles and fades. Crowd characters a scene doesn't dress are shown in the appearance their entity gives their voice tag, so they no longer come out headless. A cutscene section plays its cutscene, with its music. **Read through** now plays the whole scene that way in one click: it opens the preview, plays each section and cutscene in turn, picks a random option at each choice (or the one clicked) and a random way at each condition, until the scene ends.
- **Cutscene viewer**: faces move now: each actor's face animation (eyes, brows, expressions, nods) and each line's lip sync from the speech files, where the cutscene says it. Its 3D stage is now a control of its own, so the scene viewer's preview plays cutscenes exactly as it does.
- **Cutscene and character fixes**: through the camera, shots are framed as REDkit frames them (the camera's field of view is up the height, so they were too close before) and the aspect ratio now shows more or less at the sides; the near clipping plane follows the cutscene's clipping-plane events, with the far plane fitted to the scene so surfaces no longer show through one another. Hair and beards are cut out by their textures' alpha instead of drawn as solid cards. Vertices saved with no bone weights move with their first bone (Imlerith's face no longer stretches across the scene). The entity viewer opens characters facing you.
- **Cutscene viewer, rebuilt like REDkit's cutscene editor**: the actors are drawn as they are in the game, put together from their entities in the appearances the cutscene gives them, textured and coloured, and played on their rigs where the cutscene puts them, seen from anywhere or through the cutscene's camera in a 16:9 picture (no more stick figures), with a floor under everywhere they go. Under the view are REDkit's controls: field of view, aspect ratio, light position and which camera to look through, with the camera's own field of view and depth of field shown, and playback can loop. The player is shown in the gear Geralt's default inventory gives him (the starting armour, his hairstyle, and his swords on his back), looked up in the game's item definitions. Cutscenes play with their sound, as in REDkit: the recorded music and effects from the game's sound caches and each dialog line's voice where it's said, with buttons to turn each off, and **Save sound…** in **Dialogs** saves them as WAV files.
- **Dyed colours as the game makes them**: clothes and armour made to be dyed (their textures red and blue where the colour goes) are now coloured as the engine's colour shift does it, read from REDkit's material compiler and the game's compiled shaders (each shift a colour matrix, the red parts taking the first and the blue parts the second, kept off grey as the material says), in the cutscene and entity viewers and the preview pane. Every dye comes out as it's named, and Geralt's starting gear as it looks in the game. Dyeable items now open undyed (dye_default) rather than in the first dye listed. Material values left at their default are now read as the game reads them, which also turned on shifting for pieces such as Geralt's starting boots. Beside the timeline are REDkit's tabs: **Properties** (the event clicked), **Cutscene** (actors and their animations), **Template** (the cutscene's own properties, from REDkit's source file when it has one), **Effects**, **Animation** (each animation's properties and event track), **Dialogs** (each line with its words, speaker and sound), **Scenes** and **Camera** (field of view, position, depth of field, clipping planes and fade at the time shown), as well as **Events** and **Files**. Clicking a scene the cutscene uses now opens it in the scene viewer.
- **Properties window for every resource**: REDkit opens every resource in a property window, even with no editor for it, and now so does this tool. Double-click a layer, a grass mask, a terrain tile, a font, a particle system, a quest or any other CR2W file without a viewer of its own (or right-click any of them, **Properties**) to see every object in it, each under the one it belongs to, and the picked one's properties as REDkit lists them: from REDkit's source file for the game's own files, as in the other viewers. Files they name open from their links. Before, double-clicking these only ticked them. When REDkit's copy is in an older format the tool can't read yet (some fonts are), the note above the properties says so.
- **Property lists**: click a picked row again to unpick it, in every viewer. In the texture viewers, a file a property names (a cube map's face textures, an array's textures) opens in the texture viewer when its link is clicked.
- **Properties from REDkit's source files**: cooking leaves out most of the properties REDkit's editors show (a cooked cube map keeps none). With REDkit installed, the texture, texture array, mesh, entity and behaviour tree viewers show the properties of the game's own files from REDkit's copies in `r4data`: a cube map's strategy, compression and six faces, an array's textures, an entity's and its components' editor properties, a behaviour tree's node positions. What they draw still comes from the file the game loads. A note above the properties says where they came from, or that REDkit isn't installed, or that REDkit has no copy. Files from mods, projects or anywhere else show their own properties, as that's what's being looked at. REDkit's oldest files (CR2W version 159) can now be read too.
- **Texture viewer, more like REDkit's**: **Properties** beside the picture, listed as REDkit lists them: what the cache holds (format, size, mip levels, faces or slices, memory), then the properties of the texture's `.xbm`, `.w2cube` or `.texarray` under each class from CResource down (compression, texture group, resident mip…), with those the file doesn't keep dimmed. **3D** puts the texture on a lit sphere, box, cylinder or plane, with the light moved round by a slider, to turn and zoom; a cube map goes round the shape whole, each part showing the face it looks at.
- **Texture array viewer**: a `.texarray` opens as REDkit's texture array editor shows one: its textures in a row (`texture_0`, `texture_1`…, and how many slots are used), the one picked large, and the array's properties. Double-click a texture to open it in the texture viewer. **Save PNGs** writes every texture in it, and **Save DDS** the whole array.
- **Class filter**, as REDkit's asset browser has: **Class** beside the search box shows only the files that hold one class of resource (`CMesh`, `CBitmapTexture`, `CEntityTemplate`, `CStoryScene`, `C2dArray`…), each with how many there are in the folder picked. It works with the search, in the asset browser and any open bundle. Each class was read from the game's own files.
- **Bookmarks in the asset browser**: the star beside Class bookmarks the folder you're in, and right-click **Bookmark** bookmarks files. The bookmarks button lists them and goes straight to one, opening the asset browser if it isn't open and highlighting a bookmarked file. They're kept between sessions.
- **Cube maps and texture arrays in the texture viewer**: a `.w2cube` shows any of its six faces and a `.texarray` any of its textures (**Face** or **Slice** beside Mip), double-clicked in a texture cache or in a bundle, like an `.xbm`. **Save DDS** writes every face or slice, as a cube map or an array that texture tools open. Before this only the first slice showed, its smaller mip levels came out scrambled, and Save DDS wrote a broken file. The environment probes in layers' texture caches (`.w2l`) show as the half-float cube maps they are, instead of as one scrambled RGB32F picture.
- **Open as JSON for every CR2W file in the game**: foliage (`.flyr`), occlusion, SpeedTree (`.srt`), fur, spawn trees, cell maps, destruction, texture arrays, APEX and cloth, character templates (`.w2cent`), appearances (`.w3app`), menus, popups, the HUD, DLC descriptions and the rest now show **Open as JSON** too. Before, about 30 kinds didn't.
- **Subtitles and lists preview as text**: cutscene subtitles (`.subs`) and `.list` files open in the preview like other text files.
- **Asset Browser** (Ctrl+Shift+A, beside Open folder, or Tools): every file the game has, about 300,000 of them, in one tree to browse, search, preview and open, like WolvenKit's asset browser: content0, the DLC and the installed mods, each game path once, from where the game loads it, with textures that are only in a texture cache. **Copy to project** copies a file into the project at its game path, a mesh with its buffers. It opens in a second or two.
- **Build compiles the scripts into the mod**: Build now compiles the project's scripts into `content\precompiled.rsblob`, the way REDkit publishes a mod, and can ship the loose scripts in `content\scripts` too, as the mod's source beside the blob (with an `info.json` that has the game run the compiled ones) or on their own for the game to compile. The first build asks, and **Project properties** has both under **When the mod is built**. Scripts that don't compile stop the build, and **Show errors** lists them with links to their lines.
- **Compile moved to the Project menu**: **Project > Compile scripts** (and **Compile scripts to…**) replace the Compile button in the preview window, and Ctrl+Shift+B in a preview builds the mod, as it does in the main window.
- **Adding a script from outside the project copies it in**: **Add files to the project** on a `.ws` file from outside the scripts folder offers **Copy into scripts**, so it's compiled with the mod, instead of only linking it under **external**, where it wasn't compiled.
- **Installed mods are always searched**: go to definition, find all references, who else changes this, the type hierarchy, the usage graph and the new completion list now include every installed mod's scripts, compiled and loose, read in the background as soon as a script opens. The **Search Now**, **Loose scripts** and **Compiled scripts** offers are gone: decompiling a typical mods folder takes well under a second.
- **Names while typing in a script**: after a dot, a list of the members of the type before it and its bases (an array's built-in methods for an array); starting a name, the locals, parameters, the class's members, globals, types, enum values and keywords that fit. Enter or Tab takes the one picked, and a bracket, dot, comma or semicolon takes it and goes in after it. Ctrl+Space opens it any time; Settings turns off the list while typing.
- **Snippets** in the script editor: Ctrl+J (or a snippet's name and Tab) inserts common WitcherScript, from `@wrapMethod` and the other annotations to a statemachine, a timer, a key listener, a menu setting, a log line and a fact check, with Tab moving between the parts to fill in. Your own go in `%AppData%\W3ModWorkbench\snippets` as `.ws` files.
- **Rename** (F2) a function, event, variable, type or enum value your project declares, everywhere the project uses it. Uses are found the way Find all references finds them; open files keep the change unsaved, others are saved, and the list under the files shows every place that changed. Names the game or another mod declares can't be renamed, so nothing breaks there.
- **Console commands** (Tools): every `exec function` in the game, the installed mods and the project, searchable, with what to type in the console, its parameters and where it's declared.
- **Quest facts** (Tools): every fact the scripts set or read with `FactsAdd`, `FactsQuerySum` and the rest, with each place that does, filtered to the facts mods use, facts set but never read, read but never set, or names built in code.
- **Scripts log** (Tools): follows `Documents\The Witcher 3\scriptslog.txt` as the game writes it, filtered by words, channel or problems. Double-click a line to open the `LogChannel` call that wrote it, followed through the game's small logging functions such as `LogBoat`.
- **Key binding helper** (Tools): writes the `input.settings` lines, the `input.xml` entry and the listener script for a mod's own key, says whether the key is free in the contexts chosen, and can add it to your `input.settings` to try. The game's own `input.xml` is only changed after a separate confirmation, with a `.bak` copy.
- **Hashes and IDs** (Tools): a name's CR2W, `.w3strings` key and Wwise hashes (and FNV-1a, CRC-32), the name behind a hash from the game's scripts and string keys, a number as hex, signed and float bits, and the block of string IDs a Nexus mod ID gives.
- **CR2W and JSON** (Tools): turns the game's resource files (`.env`, `.w2mi`, `.w2ent`, quests, scenes, meshes and the rest) into JSON to edit by hand or with a script, and back, byte for byte: every file is checked to come back exactly as it was before it's written (all 870 of the game's `.env` files, and samples of 25 other kinds). Curves read as `time`, `value` and `controlPoint` instead of the engine's shortened names. **Bulk edit** makes the same edit to hundreds of files at once: copy a value from another file (the same tone mapping in every `.env`), set one, remove one, or multiply or add to the numbers under it, with **Check** to try it first and **Choose…** to pick the property from a file. **CR2W files as JSON** beside Extract to extracts them from the bundles as JSON straight away. The same runs from a command line or a Python script, with no window: `--to-json`, `--from-json` and `--edit` (`--help-json` lists the options). Thanks to Jaxonic for the idea.
- **JSON editor**: `.json` files open in an editor of their own, like the XML one, instead of failing as a bundle: coloured and folded as you type, with an outline of the objects and lists, find, Format, and a live check that points at a mistake. A file the CR2W converter made is checked to still convert back as you type, with the wrong value named if it doesn't, and **Save as name.env** writes the game file. Right-click a CR2W file in the list and **Open as JSON** to edit it straight from the bundle; Save writes it back as CR2W into your project. Drop a `.json` on the window, open it with Open files, or use **Tools › Open a JSON file…**. **Open embedded file** (Ctrl+E) opens a CR2W file kept inside another (an entity's cooked effects and streamed components) in a window of its own, and Save there writes it back into the file it came from.
- **The game's files are never changed**: one rule, everywhere in the tool. Files in bundles, texture caches and compiled scripts, every file in the game's folder, its DLC and the installed mods, and REDkit's own files are read-only, in the script, text, XML and strings editors, the mesh viewer, and every save and export. Typing in one says why and offers **Copy to project**; extracting into the game's folders, or making a project there, is refused. Before this, a script from Browse installed mods, or the game's own `.w3strings`, could be saved in place.
- **Copy to project**: right-click files in the list (or take the editor's offer) to copy them into the open project at their game path: scripts into its scripts folder, everything else into its new **workspace**, a mesh with its buffers. The copy opens, ready to change, and nothing the project has already is overwritten. With no project open it asks to make or open one.
- **Create a mod, Build and Install**: a new project now has a **mod name** (`modThisIsMyMod`, made from its name), goes in `Documents\W3 Mod Workbench` unless you choose another folder (never inside the game's), and has `scripts` and `workspace` folders. **Project > Build** (Ctrl+Shift+B) makes the mod in the project's `build` folder: scripts in `content\scripts`, the workspace packed into `blob0.bundle` and `buffers0.bundle` with its `metadata.store` by REDkit's wcc_lite, and the project's strings. **Project > Install** copies the build into the game's mods folder, replacing only that mod's folder, and asks first if a folder of that name wasn't installed from this project. Build packs and doesn't cook: before it builds it lists anything in the workspace the game wouldn't load (REDkit source files that aren't cooked, and pictures meant as textures) and asks whether to go on, and Copy to project says when a copy isn't cooked.
- **Changing materials, saved into the project**: in the mesh viewer each material's **Parameters** lists everything its shader has (textures, numbers, colours, vectors), its own and what it takes from its base, and any of them can be changed. **Save to project** (Ctrl+S) saves the changes into the open project, at each file's game path in its **workspace**, never over the files they came from: a material kept in the mesh is saved in the mesh (with its buffers), a shared `.w2mi` is either copied with the changes or replaced by a new material for this mesh, and **Other material…** is kept as the mesh pointing at that `.w2mi`. **Save as new material…** makes a new `.w2mi` in the project from any material. With no project open, it asks to make or open one. The project's files are found before the game's, so the mesh shows your version afterwards. The tool writes CR2W files itself: an unchanged file comes out byte for byte as it went in (checked on about ten thousand of the game's meshes, materials and entities and all of REDkit's meshes).
- **Mesh viewer**: double-click a `.w2mesh` (or press Enter) to see it in 3D with its textures, and the preview pane shows it too. Drag to turn it, right-drag to move it, wheel to zoom, double-click to fit; **Front**, **Side** and **Top** views, a **LOD** picker with each level's triangles and distance, **Wireframe**, and **Textures** off to see the shape alone. Each chunk can be hidden, and each material lists where it's from (in the mesh, or a `.w2mi`, down to its shader) and every texture it uses, its own and its bases'. **Colour texture** draws any of them on the mesh, **Other texture…** puts on any texture from the game or an open mod, and **Other material…** swaps in another `.w2mi`, all while you look. **Export OBJ** saves the LOD shown with its textures as PNG files. The mesh's buffers, materials and textures are found the way the game finds them, in open archives first and then in the game's bundles and texture caches (mods, DLC, content0), so a mesh in the game's own bundles shows with its textures without opening anything else. Reads the game's cooked meshes (CR2W 162 to 164, original and Remastered) and REDkit's uncooked ones, which open from Open files or by dropping them on the window. The CR2W reader is the tool's own, written with WolvenKit's sources as a guide to the format.
- **Cutscene viewer**: the preview pane shows a `.w2cutscene` at a glance (its length, its actors with the entities they're made from, a timeline of its events and the scene that plays it), and double-clicking one opens it in a window of its own. There the timeline has a lane for the whole scene and one for each actor, and zooms (Ctrl+wheel) to see events up close; every event is listed with its time, length and what it does (dialog lines, music and sounds, effects, items put in or taken from a hand, appearance changes, fades, clipping planes, depth of field, lights, cloth), searchable and narrowed to an actor or a kind. It lists the effects the cutscene defines with their particle systems, and the files it uses, which open in their viewers where the tool has one. Every cutscene in the game reads. Above the timeline the actors **play back as stick figures** on their rigs (found through their entities), with play and pause (Space), a scrub bar and a playhead on the timeline that can be dragged, seen from anywhere or **through the cutscene's camera** (C). Faces, items and effects aren't played yet. Dialog marks show their lines in words, found in the scene that plays the cutscene.
- **Mesh viewer, more like REDkit's**: an info panel (triangles, vertices, chunks, size, mesh and texture memory), a floor grid, and **Show** for the bounding box, vertex colours, normals, a skinned mesh's bones and its collision. New tabs beside Materials: **Properties** (every property, searchable), **LODs** (each LOD's distance and chunks), **Report** (memory, each material's share of each LOD, every texture with its size) and **Collision**, read from the game's collision.cache (convex and triangle mesh shapes as PhysX cooked them, with their physical materials). Material editing stays as it was.
- **Behaviour tree viewer**: double-click a `.w2behtree` to see the AI tree drawn from its root down, each node coloured by what it does (composite, decorator, condition, script task, action, subtree, comment) and saying its task, its main settings and its priority. Fold branches away (double-click a node, or fold below a level), find nodes by name, class, task or variable (Enter for the next), and click a node to see all its properties and those of the script task it runs. Trees an entity uses open from the entity viewer. Every tree in the game reads (390).
- **Entity viewer**: double-click a `.w2ent` to see the entity put together in 3D with its textures, in any of its appearances (its own meshes and those of the head, body and armour templates each appearance includes), its components laid out as REDkit's entity editor has them with the attachments between them, and every property of the component picked, the entity and the template, with its appearances, slots, effects and the files it uses. The preview pane shows the entity in 3D too. The actors in the cutscene and scene viewers open in it. Every entity in the game reads (38,619, base game and DLC). **Animations play on it**: pick one of the animation sets the entity uses and an animation, and its skinned meshes move with it on its skeleton, with play and pause (Space) and a scrub bar, centred and facing the way the entity does, in wireframe too. A face moves with the head as one, so lips no longer pull apart while an animation plays. Colours are worked out the way the game's shaders do (read from their material graphs): hair takes its colour from the mesh's vertex colours (black for Yennefer, blond for Voorhis), and a component's colour entry for the appearance shifts the red and blue parts of its texture where its tint mask says (no more placeholder red and blue sleeves), and the eyes are no longer covered by their shadow, in the viewer and the preview pane. **Cloth is drawn**: coats, skirts and capes (`.redcloth`, NVIDIA APEX clothing) are read from the game's `collision.cache`, or from the file itself for REDkit's, and shown in the shape they have before they're simulated, skinned to the character so they follow an animation.
- **Scene viewer**: double-click a `.w2scene` to see its flow: every part as a card in the order the flow reaches it, sections with their lines in words (from the game's strings, in any language) and who says them to whom, choices with each option, what it needs or does and where it leads, conditions on facts, random picks and scripts with where each way goes. Follow the links from part to part and go back (Alt+Left), search lines and speakers, and open the cutscenes, entities and dialog sets it uses. The preview pane shows a scene at a glance: its size, how it starts with each speaker in a colour of their own, who's in it, the cutscenes it plays and the facts and scripts it uses. Every scene in the game reads (6,495, base game and DLC). **Hear it**: play any line's voice, or **Read through** (R) to hear the scene from a part on, following the flow and waiting at choices and conditions for you to pick the way. The voices are read from the game's speech files and decoded by the tool itself (Wwise Opus).
- **Search game strings from REDkit's database**: **Read from** in the game strings search can now read REDkit's strings database (`LocalEditorStringDataBaseW3_UTF8.db`) instead of the game's `.w3strings`. The database names every key (the game's files only keep a hash) and says what kind of text each string is, the scene, journal or other resource it's used in, and the voiceover of spoken lines, each in a column of its own. Search finds part of a key's name, a scene path or a voiceover name, and the right-click menu copies the key or `GetLocStringByKeyExt("key")`. It's found from the wcc_lite in Settings or in the usual Steam and GOG folders, or pick any database file; installed mods' strings can be added on top. The database is read by the tool itself, so no SQLite library is needed. Thanks to glassfish for the idea.
- **More buttons in the preview's toolbar**: **Go to symbol** (Ctrl+T), **Find in files** (Ctrl+Shift+F), **Insert snippet** (Ctrl+J) and **Build** (Ctrl+Shift+B), which only had keys before, sit beside Back, Forward and History.
- **Disabled buttons look disabled** in every theme and preset. Buttons with an icon and a label kept their full colour when disabled, so the Extract buttons and Close all looked ready to click, and a disabled Save showed black text on a dark button; they now take the theme's grey.
- **Tooltips follow the theme**: every tooltip is a small rounded card in the light, dark or preset colours instead of the plain white Windows one, and long ones wrap.
- The checkbox on a thumbnail is sized with the thumbnails: smaller for small, larger for large.

## 1.6.0

- **Meshes extract with their `.buffer` files**: a mesh keeps its shape in numbered `.buffer` files, which the game packs in their own bundle (`buffers0.bundle` beside `blob0.bundle`). With **Include .buffer files** (on by default, in the extract bar) extracting a file takes its buffers along from any bundle open in the same folder, and extracting a buffer takes the file it belongs to.
- **Textures as PNG**: a new extract option writes textures from a texture cache, and `.dds` files, as full-size PNG pictures instead of `.dds`.
- **Recent folders and files**: the clock button beside Open folder lists what you opened last, and the start screen shows the latest few, to open again with a click.
- **Compare two files** (right-click, or Ctrl+D with two highlighted): text opens side by side in the preview with the differences marked; other files are compared byte for byte.
- **Other copies of this file** (right-click, or Ctrl+Shift+D): every open archive with a file at the same path, in load order, with the copy the game uses marked and whether each one's content matches. Compare, open or show any of them from there.
- **Show in Explorer** (right-click, or Shift+Alt+R, and on tree entries): opens the bundle's, cache's or loose file's folder with it selected, the way Windows' own "Open file location" does. Files you've extracted have **Show extracted file**, and **Open folder when done** now selects the new files.
- **Copy** on the right-click menu has the game path (Ctrl+C), the name (Ctrl+Shift+C), the folder and the location on disk.
- **What each file is**: resting on a file's name says what kind of file it is (mesh, data buffer, entity, quest phase, Flash panel and about fifty more), and the preview pane says so for files it can't show.
- **Export the list as CSV** on the right-click menu: the files listed, or the highlighted ones, as a spreadsheet with name, path, kind, sizes, format, mod and location.
- `.xbm` files in bundles show their texture as a thumbnail, found in a texture cache the way double-clicking one does.
- In a narrow window the toolbar keeps to one line: Settings, Help, Close all, Tools and View drop their labels before the search box gets squeezed.
- The source is sorted into folders (`Views`, `Controls`, `Services`, `Theming`, `Converters`) instead of sitting at the top of the project.
- **View menu and preview pane in the main window**: **View** switches the file list between **Details** and small, medium or large **thumbnails** (textures and picture files show their picture, other files an icon for their kind; click, Ctrl+click, Shift+click and the arrow keys select, double-click opens). **Preview pane** (Alt+P) shows the highlighted file beside the list: textures and pictures as images (an `.xbm` in a bundle finds its texture in the cache), scripts with WitcherScript colouring, XML with XML colouring, and other text as plain text. The choice and the pane's width are remembered.
- **Picture files open in the texture viewer**: PNG, JPEG, BMP, GIF, TIFF and DDS files in bundles (and in projects) show in the same viewer as cached textures, with channels, zoom and Save PNG. A DDS keeps its mip levels. Double-click, or press Enter.
- **Texture caches** (`texture.cache`): open the game's, a DLC's or a mod's texture cache like a bundle (Open files, Open folder, Browse installed mods) and every texture in it is listed under its game path, with its format and size. A 30 GB cache opens in a moment, since only its index is read. Double-click a texture, or press Enter, to see it in a viewer with its mip levels, the colour and each channel (R, G, B, A) on their own, a chequerboard for transparency, fit or actual size (Ctrl+wheel zooms), and **Save PNG** and **Save DDS**. Extracting a texture writes a `.dds` with all its mip levels. Reads BC1, BC3, BC4, BC5 and BC7 (the formats the cache uses for colour, normals, masks and the Remastered's high quality textures), plain 32-bit colour and float data. Arrays and cube maps show their first slice.
- **`.xbm` files in bundles open their texture**: an `.xbm` in a bundle only describes the texture, so double-clicking one finds its pixels in a texture cache under the same path (an open one, the one beside the bundle, or the game's) and shows them in the viewer.
- **The tree groups a mod's files under the mod's name**: a mod's bundles, compiled scripts, texture cache, strings and loose scripts are now listed under one entry for the mod (Browse installed mods, Open folder), instead of as separate top-level items. Selecting the mod shows every file in it, and right-click closes or extracts all of it. A mod with one archive stays as it was.
- **Real projects**: a project is now a `.w3bench` file (plain JSON) in the mod's folder, not just any folder of scripts you pointed the tool at. It names the mod's **scripts folder**, lists the **other files and folders** that belong to the mod (XML, strings, notes), remembers **where Compile writes** the `.rsblob`, and carries a name, author, version and description. Paths are relative, so a project can be moved or kept in git. **Project** in the toolbar has **New project**, **Open project** (Ctrl+Shift+O), **Recent projects**, **Project properties**, **Add files / a folder to the project**, **Refresh** and **Close project**. A `.w3bench` file opens by dropping it on the window or through Open files, and so does a folder with one in it. Project properties edit everything in it, and Compile's output now lives in the project (old per-folder choices in settings are picked up when a project is made for that folder).
- **One workspace project**: one project is open at a time and every window knows it. It is the first entry in the tree (PROJECT badge, the project's name), showing its scripts and other files laid out as in the mod's folder; XML files in it open in the XML editor. Opening another project, closing it or closing the tool asks about unsaved files. The tool **reopens the last project** with the scripts that were open (kept in the tool's settings folder, not in the project file; turn it off in Settings).
- **Loose files ask to join**: scripts opened outside the open project (Open folder, a dropped `.ws`, Find in files) are *loose scripts*, listed without a badge. They can be read and followed, but typing in one stops and asks: **Add to project** (with a project open) or **Create project…** from the script's folder (with none), or edit without. A file edited outside a project says **not in a project** and has no Compile button. The XML and strings editors ask once per session, after a save, whether to add the file. Right-click files for **Add to project** / **Remove from project** (the file is never deleted), and right-click loose scripts for **Create a project from these scripts**.
- **Edit as project** on a decompiled mod now makes a `.w3bench` project in the folder you pick. Find in files' scope is **The open project and loose scripts**.
- **Built-in script compiler**: Compile no longer needs REDkit. When wcc_lite isn't set, it compiles the project with a compiler built into the tool, which reads the game's own scripts (from the game folder, or REDkit's if found) and the mods you have installed (so a project built on Shared Utils finds it), and writes the same kind of `.rsblob` in about a second. On the NG mods it was tried on, the blobs are byte-for-byte what wcc_lite writes or the same code, declaration by declaration, and on about ten thousand of the game's own functions 99.6% came out the same. **Always use the built-in compiler** in Settings picks it even when wcc_lite is set. Maps (`map<K, V>`) work in both the compiler and the decompiler, which no longer stops on compiled scripts that use them. It is a port of the compiler webspam wrote for Aelto's tw3-cahirb (pull request 1).
- **View XML overrides** (Tools): lists the definitions (items, abilities, sets…) that mod.io mods, DLC and mods set in their XML, both loose files and XML packed in bundles, normal and New Game+ kept apart. Definitions with `on_conflict` (the Remastered's XML overrides) and plain ones that redefine the same name are both counted. Script Merger's merged mod is understood: its `MergeInventory.xml` (found beside the configured merger, or in the usual places) says which mods went into each merged file, so the merged copy is labelled "merge of …" and those mods' own copies are marked as already inside it instead of being reported as clashes. The rules follow Script Merger Remastered: a field only clashes when two mods give it different values, and the winner is decided by the game's XML order (mod.io mods first, then DLC by name with the later name winning). For each one it shows the mods that touch it, winner first, whether each extends or replaces it, the fields they set, and which mod wins each field when several set the same one. Open any of the files from the list.
- The **load order analyser** now includes mods installed through the in-game mod.io hub. They live outside the game folder (the root is read from `%LOCALAPPDATA%\mod.io\globalsettings.json`, by default `C:\Users\Public\mod.io`) and load before every other mod, so they come first in the order and are checked for conflicts like the rest.
- Tool windows (preview, XML editor, load order analyser, find in files, strings, stub generator, usage graph, help…) are now **independent windows**: each has its own taskbar button and no longer minimises, hides or closes with the main window.
- **Show usage graph** on the script right-click menu: right-click a function, event, variable or type and get a graph of how it's used. The thing itself is on the right, the functions that use it are to its left, and the functions that use those further left, up to six levels out. Click a box to light up its chain of calls down to the target, double-click it to open it (Ctrl for the other side), and hover or click the label on a line to see the lines of code that make the call. Right-click a box to start a new graph from it. Drag with either mouse button to pan and Ctrl+wheel to zoom; **Fit** shows the whole graph and **Stretch** fills the window. Steps with many callers wrap into several columns. It offers to search the installed mods' loose and compiled scripts when they aren't included yet, so callers in other mods show up too.
- Usage graph boxes have a third line in the accent colour naming the mod the script is from, or **Game Script** for the game's own scripts.
- Go to definition, find usages, find all references and the usage graph now **prefer a mod's compiled scripts over its loose ones**. A mod that ships both is listed once, from the compiled blob; mods without a blob fall back to their loose scripts.
- Fixed: an error while the window was being laid out could hang the tool, using a whole CPU core, instead of showing the error. Seen when clicking a thumbnail after previewing a script.
- Fixed: in the stub generator, switching between Wrap, Replace and the other kinds did nothing until you left it and came back. The output now updates straight away and keeps the selected function.
- The menu builder's **Script** tab now has WitcherScript syntax highlighting and line numbers.

## 1.5.1

- **Load order analyser** (Tools, Ctrl+Shift+L): reads every installed mod and DLC and checks the load order. Compiled scripts are decompiled to what they declare and use, loose scripts parsed, bundle tables and strings listed, and priorities come from `mods.settings` (DLC first, then mods by priority, then unlisted mods by folder name).
  - **Collisions**: loose scripts at the same path, `@replaceMethod`, `@addMethod` and `@addField` used twice, replaces mixed with wraps, wraps stacking, classes, global functions and enum values declared twice, bundle files with different content, and string IDs with different text.
  - **Breakages**: annotations whose class or function the game and the other mods don't have (usually a mod made for another game version), and mods that use something no installed mod provides.
  - **Dependencies and order**: a mod that needs another mod that is missing or disabled; and, because scripts compile in reverse load order, a dependency such as Shared Utils that loads *before* the mods using it, or has a priority when they don't (or the other way round). Dependencies declared in `info.json` are checked too. Leftover `mods.settings` entries for removed mods are flagged.
  - **Who wins**: clashes with a clear outcome show a ★ winner on the row and in a WINNER box, and the winner is starred in the file lists. A merged-files mod that loads first is treated as the answer to the clashes it covers.
  - Pick a mod to see only its findings; filter by Problems, Warnings and Notes; **Save report** writes Markdown.
- **Script merger** setting (Settings): the path to Script Merger Remastered by relapse12. The analyser suggests it for script and bundle clashes and has an **Open Script Merger** button. The merger is a separate download and is only started, never bundled.
- Help: a new **Load order analyser** page, and the Settings and shortcuts pages cover the new setting and Ctrl+Shift+L.
