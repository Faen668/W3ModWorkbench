# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Bestiary** (**Tools > Bestiary…**): the game's bestiary with its own artwork and icons, category by category: each creature's page (what it's weak to, with the oils', bombs' and signs' icons, its description, vital spots and hints), every place in the worlds that spawns it on the game's map, coloured by level, with each place's kind, spawn limit and how many at once, its kinds with their levels and abilities, and what each kind drops with its chances (double-click a drop to find it in the Loot Locator). Creatures are matched to their entries by the same tests the game makes when you meet them. Creatures that only appear in quests say so; animals, people and others the bestiary has no entry for can be listed too. **Export** saves the places as CSV; **Settings > Viewers** sets the defaults.
- **Loot Locator** (**Tools > Loot Locator…**): search for an item, by its in-game name or its name in the files, or for a loot table, and the game's own map shows every placed container, herb and beehive that can give it, coloured by its chance at the player level chosen. Each place lists its tables and items with their chances, its respawn time and position, and what the game's roll gives that exact spot; a table shows what it gives each time it's used. **Export** saves every place as CSV; **Installed mods** reads mods' layers and templates as the game would. The places come from the worlds' layers (49,806 in nine worlds, the same tables and positions as REDkit on 64 checked), the chances from the game's loot roll re-created from `witcher3.exe`: table weights of 100 when unset, one table a param unless set, chance -1 items first and evenly, the rest by weight with none twice, level limits, and the roll seeded by where a place stands, so the same spot always gets the same items. **Settings > Viewers** sets the starting level, whether mods are read and whether each spot's rolled items show.
- The World window's **Game map** is sharp up close: zooming in past the whole map's own detail draws the game's full-size minimap tiles (512 or 1,024 pixels each, about four times as sharp) for the part in view. It also finds the minimaps of worlds kept in a DLC's own folder, such as Toussaint's, and of worlds whose folder and file names differ, such as the Spiral's.
- **Lip sync made from a recording, learnt from your own game**: packing voice lines (the Voice lines window's **Make a lip sync for each line that has none, from its sound**, on unless unticked) and **Build** give every line with a `.wav` or `.wem` but no lip sync one made from its sound. The first time, the app learns how the face's controls move with the sound from the game's own lines on your computer (10,000 of them with the lip syncs CDPR made, about ten minutes; **Learn again** does it again), and keeps what it learnt in your settings folder: nothing outside the app's own code, and nothing of the game's copied anywhere. It hears the sound, not the words, so any language works. On lines it hadn't heard it follows CDPR's lip sync with a median correlation of 0.59 (the jaw 0.65), and each control is calibrated so its movements are as big as the game's. `--lipsync-from-audio`, `--learn-lipsync` and `--speech-pack --make-lipsync` do the same on the command line; **Settings > Viewers** turns it off. Not yet seen in the game.
- Fixed: scenes from Blood and Wine and Hearts of Stone stood actors inside one another when they played the expansion's own dialog animations (q701_21_duchess_after_fight: Guillaume in Palmerin as Palmerin lifts him). The expansions add animation sets to the game's base characters as the game loads them (their .reddlc's entity template mounters); the preview now reads those too, so men, women and children find the expansions' animations.
- Scene voice lines start in time with the playhead: each line is decoded while the one before it plays, and started by the output's delay ahead, instead of a little behind.
- Faster scenes and cutscenes: playing one redrew its whole timeline (every track, event and label) for every frame just to move the playhead, which held q703_02_at_castel_ravello down to about 25 frames a second. Only the playhead is drawn again now, and the scene plays at the display's 60.
- Faster scenes: the floor grid under a scene or cutscene is now cut to the camera by moving its lines in place, only when the camera moves. It used to build new line buffers for every frame played through the camera.
- **Frame rate in the 3D views**: **Settings > Viewers > Show the frame rate in 3D views** puts a small panel in the corner of every 3D view (meshes, entities, cutscenes, scenes, the world and the map): frames a second, how long a frame takes on average and at the slowest over the last second, and in the world view how long the graphics card takes to draw one. Open views follow the setting at once.
- **A project's files handled as in Explorer**: in the main window's file list, a project's own files can be cut, copied and pasted ({{Ctrl+X}}, {{Ctrl+C}}, {{Ctrl+V}}, into the folder picked in the tree, and to and from Explorer), duplicated, renamed ({{F2}}) and deleted to the Recycle Bin ({{Del}}, asking first unless **Settings > General** says not to). A file pasted where it is gets a copy's name; one whose name is taken asks whether to replace it or keep both. **Refresh the project** ({{F5}}) keeps the folders open and the one picked, where it used to fold the whole tree up, and so does every change made here. **View > Files in subfolders too**, and the folder button beside **View**, switch a folder between every file under it and only its own, as Explorer lists them; a search still looks in every folder under it. Right-clicking a folder of the project, or of loose files opened from a folder, offers **Convert to JSON** and **Convert JSON back**: every CR2W file in it and its subfolders gets its `.json` beside it (each checked to convert back to the same bytes), or every converted `.json` its file back, the originals kept; files already there are asked about once, and **Cancel** on the status bar stops it.
- Fixed: dyed gear in the 3D views (Geralt's starting armour in cutscenes) showed small flat squares of green, orange or pink on brown leather. Our block compressor, which packs repainted textures for the graphics card and for the packer, sometimes fitted a 4×4 block along the wrong colour line: one whose red and blue go opposite ways, as brown leather beside blue-grey cloth does. On the viper armour's three textures no block now comes out more than 33 off (the worst was 141).
- **Your own voice recordings go in the game**: a voice line folder's `ID.wav` with no `ID.wem` beside it is made a `.wem` as it's packed, in the Voice lines window and by **Build**, as the game's voice lines are made (Wwise Opus at 48 kHz, every packet one 20 ms CELT frame at full bandwidth, the same look-ahead), so the game plays it as one of its own. Mono, stereo and 5.1, any sample rate, 8 to 32-bit PCM or float, as Audacity, Reaper and recorders save them. **Settings > Viewers** sets the bitrate (64 kbit/s a channel, near the game's own), and `--wav-to-wem` makes `.wem` files on the command line. Our own writer gives back every one of the game's 64,509 voice sounds without markers byte for byte from its own packets; the encoding is Opus's own, from the Concentus library the app already used to play voice lines. Brothers in Arms' 574 English lines made from WAV and packed all play back as long as their recordings, with their lip syncs unchanged. Not yet heard in the game.

Checked by running REDkit's own plugin on Geralt's head from REDkit: it opens our `.re` files and sets all 145 shape keys at every frame to the game's own values.

- **Lip syncs as REDkit's `.re`, for editing in Blender**: the Voice lines window's **Save lip syncs as .re, for editing in Blender** unpacks each line's lip sync as an `ID.re`, the HDF5 file REDkit's Blender plugin opens and saves, and packing or **Build** turns an `ID.re` back into the game's own lip sync. Older mods' CR2W lip syncs convert too. Our own HDF5 reader and writer, laid out as h5py writes REDkit's files: REDkit's three `.re` files read and write back with every value the same. Lines made `.re` and packed again (1,500 of the game's own, all 573 of a Brothers in Arms speech file) keep their sounds byte for byte and every face control within 0.0005 at every frame. Our lip sync writer, checked on all 64,709 of the game's lip syncs, writes each one back byte for byte. **Settings > Viewers** sets whether the box starts ticked.
- **Mesh particles play in the entity window's 3D view**: an emitter that draws its particles as meshes (a quarter of the game's: a place of power's fire as it's used, debris, leaves, shards) draws each particle as its mesh, turned as the emitter says (spinning, along its flight or not at all), sized on all three axes and coloured over its life, with its texture's atlas frame and scroll; fire and glow add light in the particle's colour as the game's effect materials do. A spawner that names a slot or a bone rather than a component (a monster's hit effects) now plays there, not at the entity's feet.

- **Voice lines** (**Tools > Voice lines (.w3speech)…**): a speech file (`enpc.w3speech` and the other languages') unpacks into a folder of sounds and lip syncs, `ID.wem` with `ID.raw` (or `ID.cr2w` in older files) and a `.wav` of each if wanted, and a folder packs back into a speech file, with the lines already in one kept if wanted. - **Entity effects play in the 3D view**: **Effects** on the entity window's toolbar lists the entity's effects (a place of power's swirling smoke, a monster's sparks, a torch's flames), each with what it's made of, and ticking one plays it: its particle systems run where their spawners put them, on a component, a slot or a bone, following the animation, at its speed. A looped effect keeps going and a one-off plays again after a moment; **All off** stops them. Particles are drawn with the view's own particle shader, blended as the game blends them (smoke covers what's behind it, fire adds to it), turned and sized each, with their atlas frames; an emitter that leaves its particles where they're born does, so they trail behind a moving bone. - **CR2W and JSON can take only some of a folder's files**: **Only files matching** and **Skip files matching** on the **Convert** and **Bulk edit** tabs are regular expressions on each file's path below its folder (`\.w2scene$` or `.*w2scene` for scenes, `^quests/`, `(^|/)thumbnails/`), with a bad pattern said as it's typed. Files they leave out are never opened, so a narrow pattern on r4data is quick. Recipes keep them and show them in their steps, and the exported scripts pass them on. On the command line they're `--match` and `--skip` (with `--recipe`, in place of the recipe's own). **`--help`**, `-h` and `/?` now print the command line's help instead of opening the window, and `--help <topic>` (commands, options, filter, edits, recipes, examples) prints one part; `--help-json` still works.

Pictures, sounds, text and models are passed over by their extension without being opened. r4data's 199,105 CR2W files are found in under a minute. A bulk edit looks through its folders once instead of twice, **Choose…** no longer freezes the window looking for a file in a big folder, and the command line shows the same progress in a terminal.

Arrays and cube maps show their first slice.
- **`.xbm` files in bundles open their texture**: an `.xbm` in a bundle only describes the texture, so double-clicking one finds its pixels in a texture cache under the same path (an open one, the one beside the bundle, or the game's) and shows them in the viewer.
- **The tree groups a mod's files under the mod's name**: a mod's bundles, compiled scripts, texture cache, strings and loose scripts are now listed under one entry for the mod (Browse installed mods, Open folder), instead of as separate top-level items. Selecting the mod shows every file in it, and right-click closes or extracts all of it. A mod with one archive stays as it was.
- **Real projects**: a project is now a `.w3bench` file (plain JSON) in the mod's folder, not just any folder of scripts you pointed the tool at. It names the mod's **scripts folder**, lists the **other files and folders** that belong to the mod (XML, strings, notes), remembers **where Compile writes** the `.rsblob`, and carries a name, author, version and description. Paths are relative, so a project can be moved or kept in git. **Project** in the toolbar has **New project**, **Open project** (Ctrl+Shift+O), **Recent projects**, **Project properties**, **Add files / a folder to the project**, **Refresh** and **Close project**. A `.w3bench` file opens by dropping it on the window or through Open files, and so does a folder with one in it. Project properties edit everything in it, and Compile's output now lives in the project (old per-folder choices in settings are picked up when a project is made for that folder).
- **One workspace project**: one project is open at a time and every window knows it. It is the first entry in the tree (PROJECT badge, the project's name), showing its scripts and other files laid out as in the mod's folder; XML files in it open in the XML editor. Opening another project, closing it or closing the tool asks about unsaved files. The tool **reopens the last project** with the scripts that were open (kept in the tool's settings folder, not in the project file; turn it off in Settings).
- **Loose files ask to join**: scripts opened outside the open project (Open folder, a dropped `.ws`, Find in files) are *loose scripts*, listed without a badge. They can be read and followed, but typing in one stops and asks: **Add to project** (with a project open) or **Create project…** from the script's folder (with none), or edit without. A file edited outside a project says **not in a project** and has no Compile button. The XML and strings editors ask once per session, after a save, whether to add the file. Right-click files for **Add to project** / **Remove from project** (the file is never deleted), and right-click loose scripts for **Create a project from these scripts**.
- **Edit as project** on a decompiled mod now makes a `.w3bench` project in the folder you pick. Find in files' scope is **The open project and loose scripts**.
- **Built-in script compiler**: Compile no longer needs REDkit. When wcc_lite isn't set, it compiles the project with a compiler built into the tool, which reads the game's own scripts (from the game folder, or REDkit's if found) and the mods you have installed (so a project built on Shared Utils finds it), and writes the same kind of `.rsblob` in about a second. On the NG mods it was tried on, the blobs are byte-for-byte what wcc_lite writes or the same code, declaration by declaration, and on about ten thousand of the game's own functions 99.6% came out the same. **Always use the built-in compiler** in Settings picks it even when wcc_lite is set. Maps (`map<K, V>`) work in both the compiler and the decompiler, which no longer stops on compiled scripts that use them. It is a port of the compiler webspam wrote for Aelto's tw3-cahirb (pull request 1).
- **View XML overrides** (Tools): lists the definitions (items, abilities, sets…) that mod.io mods, DLC and mods set in their XML, both loose files and XML packed in bundles, normal and New Game+ kept apart. Definitions with `on_conflict` (the Remastered's XML overrides) and plain ones that redefine the same name are both counted. Script Merger's merged mod is understood: its `MergeInventory.xml` (found beside the configured merger, or in the usual places) says which mods went into each merged file, so the merged copy is labelled "merge of …" and those mods' own copies are marked as already inside it instead of being reported as clashes. The rules follow Script Merger Remastered: a field only clashes when two mods give it different values, and the winner is decided by the game's XML order (mod.io mods first, then DLC by name with the later name winning). For each one it shows the mods that touch it, winner first, whether each extends or replaces it, the fields they set, and which mod wins each field when several set the same one. Open any of the files from the list.
- The **load order analyser** now includes mods installed through the in-game mod.io hub. They live outside the game folder (the root is read from `%LOCALAPPDATA%\mod.io\globalsettings.json`, by default `C:\Users\Public\mod.io`) and load before every other mod, so they come first in the order and are checked for conflicts like the rest.
- Tool windows (preview, XML editor, load order analyser, find in files, strings, stub generator, usage graph, help…) are now **independent windows**: each has its own taskbar button and no longer minimises, hides or closes with the main window.
- **Show usage graph** on the script right-click menu: right-click a function, event, variable or type and get a graph of how it's used. The thing itself is on the right, the functions that use it are to its left, and the functions that use those further left, up to six levels out. Click a box to light up its chain of calls down to the target, double-click it to open it (Ctrl for the other side), and hover or click the label on a line to see the lines of code that make the call. Right-click a box to start a new graph from it. Drag with either mouse button to pan and Ctrl+wheel to zoom; **Fit** shows the whole graph and **Stretch** fills the window. ## 1.5.1

- **Load order analyser** (Tools, Ctrl+Shift+L): reads every installed mod and DLC and checks the load order. Compiled scripts are decompiled to what they declare and use, loose scripts parsed, bundle tables and strings listed, and priorities come from `mods.settings` (DLC first, then mods by priority, then unlisted mods by folder name).
  - **Collisions**: loose scripts at the same path, `@replaceMethod`, `@addMethod` and `@addField` used twice, replaces mixed with wraps, wraps stacking, classes, global functions and enum values declared twice, bundle files with different content, and string IDs with different text.
  - **Breakages**: annotations whose class or function the game and the other mods don't have (usually a mod made for another game version), and mods that use something no installed mod provides.
  - **Dependencies and order**: a mod that needs another mod that is missing or disabled; and, because scripts compile in reverse load order, a dependency such as Shared Utils that loads *before* the mods using it, or has a priority when they don't (or the other way round). Dependencies declared in `info.json` are checked too. Leftover `mods.settings` entries for removed mods are flagged.
  - **Who wins**: clashes with a clear outcome show a ★ winner on the row and in a WINNER box, and the winner is starred in the file lists. A merged-files mod that loads first is treated as the answer to the clashes it covers.
  - Pick a mod to see only its findings; filter by Problems, Warnings and Notes; **Save report** writes Markdown.
- **Script merger** setting (Settings): the path to Script Merger Remastered by relapse12. The analyser suggests it for script and bundle clashes and has an **Open Script Merger** button. The merger is a separate download and is only started, never bundled.
